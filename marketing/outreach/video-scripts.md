**Vera, 26 Nov 2026: PASS, 22 claims, all traced to this site's published pages** (ai-scribe wasn't attached, so nothing was checked against product code). One open label: if the app's Documents view shows a Beta badge, leave it visible on screen in the pediatrics video.

**Use:** four short videos Jake records himself, talking to camera with a screen-share of
Stream. Each one sits on a page linked from a personal email from Jake
(`clinician-intro.md`), and that page carries the booking link. The viewer already clicked
because of Jake, they're sceptical, and they're giving it 90 seconds.

**One idea, in all four:** your EHR keeps the record; Stream keeps the project. That project
is the problem-by-problem picture good clinicians keep by hand. It works, but it doesn't scale,
it doesn't transfer, and it takes upkeep. Each script opens on that segment's version of the
problem. Features only come in once the problem is on the table.

**Format:** `[SCREEN: …]` lines say what is on screen. Everything else is spoken. Each
spoken section runs about 180 to 215 words, which is 75 to 90 seconds at a normal pace.

## Rules for all four recordings

- **Demo data only on screen.** Use the demo practice and demo patients. No real chart, no
  real name, no real date of birth, not even blurred. Close the inbox and any other tabs first.
- **One ask:** book 20 minutes. Don't mention the trial, a price or a discount.
- **Don't say, even ad-libbing:**
  - hours or minutes saved, "faster", "get home on time", "in minutes"
  - HIPAA, SOC 2, "secure", "compliant"
  - "integrates with", "syncs to", "writes back to" your EHR. The line is "works alongside
    any EHR", and the note pastes in.
  - "your EHR can't…" The comparison is to the system you keep by hand, not to the EHR.
  - "thinks like a clinician". If you need that idea, say "organized like clinicians think".
  - the word "narrative"
  - anything about what happens to the recording: not that it "always" stays with its visit,
    and not that every word is kept
  - task or referral tracking "closing the loop". Tracking each plan item through to completion
    is early access and isn't in these scripts.
  - customer names, practices or quotes
- **Patients in the spoken lines are composites.** Nothing in them should identify anyone.

## (a) Independent primary care: covering for a partner

**Title:** The plan was in your partner's head

**The problem it leads with:** When you cover for a partner, you're decoding their notes. The
system they keep by hand works for them, and it doesn't transfer to you.

**Script**

[SCREEN: Jake to camera, no screen-share yet.]

It's Thursday, and you're covering your partner's clinic. First patient: CKD, diabetes, three
blood pressure medications. What was the plan for the kidneys?

Your partner knows. It's in their head, and in a running problem list at the top of their
notes, in their shorthand. That system works. Most good clinicians I know keep one. But it
doesn't transfer. You start from their notes, not their system.

[SCREEN: Stream, demo patient, chart open on the problem list.]

Stream builds that system as a side effect of documenting. It's an AI scribe, and every visit's
Assessment and Plan is filed under its problem.

[SCREEN: click into CKD. Plans in date order, the author shown on each entry.]

So when you cover, you open CKD and read every plan written for it, in order, with who wrote
each one. It's the same view your partner sees. And it's your practice's own chart, not AI
output.

[SCREEN: Huddle for the day's schedule.]

Before clinic, Huddle shows each patient's active problems, and the ones nobody has touched in
ten months or more.

[SCREEN: copy the finished note, paste it into a plain text field.]

It works alongside any EHR. It doesn't integrate or write back. The note pastes in, and your
EHR stays the record.

[SCREEN: Jake to camera.]

Your EHR keeps the record. Stream keeps the project. If covering feels like decoding, book
20 minutes with me. The link is on this page.

**Notes for recording**

- Length: about 195 words, 75 to 85 seconds. If it runs long, cut the Huddle line first.
- Show: a demo patient with three or four problems and at least two authors on the CKD
  timeline, so "who wrote each one" is visible. Spend most of the screen time on that click.
- Don't say: that Stream "updates the chart automatically" or "remembers everything" (older
  primary care page copy, not claims to repeat), or any time figure for pre-charting. Don't
  add the follow-up task list. It pulls toward plan tracking, which is early access.

## (b) DPC / membership practices: the relationship is the record

**Title:** Six years with one patient

**The problem it leads with:** In a membership practice the relationship is the asset, and its
history lives in your head and in a summary you carry forward. That holds for the patients you
see often. It goes stale for the rest, and a new partner inherits your notes, not your system.

**Script**

[SCREEN: Jake to camera.]

You've had this patient six years. You know the taper that finally worked, the A1c you've been
chasing since year two, why she stopped the statin. You built a practice so you'd have the time
to know that.

Most of it lives in your head, and in a summary you carry forward at the top of every note. It
works for the patients you see often. The one you haven't seen in eight months is the one whose
summary is stale. And a new partner inherits your notes, not your system.

[SCREEN: Stream, demo patient, problem list.]

Stream is an AI scribe that keeps that system for you. Every visit's Assessment and Plan is
filed under its problem, so after a year you have a living problem list, not a stack of
disconnected documents.

[SCREEN: open one problem. Every A&P for it in order, then click Recap.]

Open a problem and read every plan you've written for it, in order. Recap adds a summary of how
it's evolved when you want one.

[SCREEN: copy the note, paste into a plain text field.]

It works alongside any EHR, or on its own. No integration, no write-back. The note pastes in.

[SCREEN: Jake to camera.]

This isn't about shorter visits. It's about walking into the long ones already knowing where you
left off. Your EHR keeps the record. Stream keeps the project. If that's how you practice, book
20 minutes with me. The link is on this page.

**Notes for recording**

- Length: about 215 words, 80 to 90 seconds.
- Show: a demo patient with a multi-year timeline. One problem with several plans over time
  (a taper, or an A1c trend) carries the whole video. Open Recap last, and make it clear it's
  optional.
- Don't say: anything about time saved, more patients or a bigger panel. These clinicians built
  their practice around time with patients, and an offer to shorten visits reads as undoing it.
  The "isn't about shorter visits" line is there on purpose, so keep it. Don't bring up coding
  or HCC capture either. It's on the DPC page, but it's a different pitch.

## (d) Post-acute (SNF / LTC): the admission that started somewhere else

**Title:** Every admission started somewhere else

**The problem it leads with:** Each admission arrives as a stack of outside records, and then
the resident is seen by several clinicians across shifts. The picture gets rebuilt from
scratch, and it gets lost at handoff.

**Script**

[SCREEN: Jake to camera.]

A new admission arrives with a forty-page discharge summary, consult notes and labs. Before you
can plan anything, someone has to read it and rebuild the chart. Then the resident is seen by
several clinicians across the week, and each one starts from the last note.

That's the part that gets lost at handoff. Good clinicians patch it with a running list in
their notes. It works for the patients they follow. It doesn't transfer to whoever's on next.

[SCREEN: Stream, upload a demo discharge summary. The extracted problems and medications appear
on a problem-oriented chart.]

With Stream, you upload the discharge summary or other outside records, and it extracts the
problems, medications and history into a chart organized by problem, from the day of admission.

[SCREEN: open the heart failure problem. Plans in order, the author on each entry.]

On rounds you record or dictate, and each visit's Assessment and Plan is filed under its
problem. Whoever rounds next opens heart failure and reads every plan for it, in order, with who
wrote each one.

[SCREEN: copy the note, paste into a plain text field.]

It works alongside any EHR. It doesn't integrate or write back. The note pastes in, and your
EHR stays the record.

[SCREEN: Jake to camera.]

Your EHR keeps the record. Stream keeps the project. If your handoffs run on someone's memory,

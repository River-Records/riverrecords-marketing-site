# Competitor Battle Cards

**What this is:** one card per competitor a Stream prospect is likely to name, written for someone on a live call. Each card says who they are, where they genuinely beat us, where we win, the one question that opens the gap, and what to do if the prospect is already paying them.

**Companion doc:** [`content-objection-map.md`](./content-objection-map.md). These cards handle the competitor *in the call*; the objection map tells you which post to send *after* it. Category 1 ("we already have a scribe") is the follow-up bucket for almost every card here.

**Pricing verified:** 2026-08-19, from public pricing pages and third-party reviews (sources at the bottom). **Re-check quarterly** — Heidi moved its clinician plan from ~$99 to $150 in February 2026 and Nabla went from public pricing to contract-only in the same window. Quoting a stale competitor price on a call costs you the room.

---

## Read this before the cards

**Every product below is a scribe. That is the whole argument.**

We do not win a scribe-versus-scribe comparison. Several of these are cheaper, some are free, most are more mature, and two have real EHR write-back that we don't have. If a rep tries to win on transcription accuracy or note quality, they are fighting on the one axis where we are — at best — even, against companies with more funding and more users.

We win by changing the question from *"which scribe is better"* to *"what happens after the note."* Every card below ends in the same place: the competitor's answer is "nothing, it's in your EHR."

### The three questions that work on any of them

Ask these instead of pitching. They're diagnostic, not rhetorical — if the prospect has good answers, we may genuinely not be a fit, and you should find that out in minute five rather than minute forty.

1. **"What happens to the note after you paste it?"** — Every scribe's honest answer is "nothing." The note is text in an EHR field. Nothing is organized, nothing carries forward, nothing is queryable.
2. **"Who reads the faxes?"** — No scribe on this list touches inbound documents. Discharge summaries, consult letters, and outside records are somebody's manual pile. This is the pain that isn't being sold to.
3. **"How do you know which of your patients are overdue for something?"** — The answer is a spreadsheet, a report nobody opens, or nobody. Again, no scribe addresses this.

### What we never claim, on any call

Full list in `docs/internal/field-sheets/` (Guardrails sheet). The three that will come up in competitive conversations specifically:

- **Never** "integrates with your EHR," "syncs," "connects to," or "writes back." We removed these from the website in August 2026 because they weren't true. Freed Premier and Suki *do* have EHR push. If we claim it too, we lose the one thing that makes our version credible — say "works alongside any EHR, nothing to install, no IT project."
- **Never** SOC 2. We don't have it. Several competitors do, and it will come up. Say what's true: BAA included on every plan, per-tenant database isolation, role-based access, audit logging, two-factor. Then move.
- **Never** invent a time-saved statistic. We have named references and 30,000+ documented encounters; use those.

## Freed

**Price:** $39 Starter (40 notes/mo) · $79 Core (unlimited) · $119 Premier (adds EHR push + ICD-10). Per clinician, no team tier.
**Wins with:** solo clinicians and 1–3 provider practices who want a scribe and nothing else. Enormous installed base among independent docs.
**Their pitch:** fast, cheap, dead simple, sign up in five minutes.

**Where they genuinely beat us**
- Price. $79 unlimited against our $149 is a real gap and you should not pretend otherwise.
- Simplicity. There is almost nothing to learn. Our seven-step setup is heavier.
- Maturity and word-of-mouth. In primary care Facebook groups, Freed is the default recommendation.
- Premier has EHR push. We have none.

**Where we win**
- Their note is a document. Ours is a chart — problem cards that persist, a Timeline per problem, a Recap across visits.
- They have no answer at all for inbound documents, tasks, care gaps, or HCC.
- After two years of Freed, the prospect has ~10,000 unstructured notes and no structure. That's the argument.

**The question:** *"After two years and ten thousand Freed notes — what can you actually do with them?"*

**If they already use Freed:** don't attack it. It's a good scribe and saying otherwise costs credibility. Position Stream as the thing Freed was never trying to be: *"Keep the note fast. The question is what the chart looks like in year three."* Best follow-up sends are objection-map category 1, ⭐ *The Note Is Solved. The Chart Is Not.*

**Note:** we have a full comparison page at `/comparison/freedai/`. Send it after the call, not during.

## Heidi

**Price:** Free tier ($0, unlimited basic consults and dictation, 10 "Pro Actions"/mo) · Clinician ~$150/user/mo billed annually · Practice and Enterprise custom.
**Wins with:** price-sensitive clinicians globally, and anyone who wants to try a scribe at zero risk.
**Their pitch:** genuinely free to start, strong templating, big international footprint.

**Where they genuinely beat us**
- **A real free tier.** This is the hardest competitive fact on this page. "Why would I pay $149 when Heidi is free?" is a fair question and needs a real answer, not a dodge.
- Template flexibility and a polished product.

**Where we win**
- Free Heidi is a scribe with no chart. Same category argument as Freed, just at $0.
- Their Clinician plan is now ~$150 — *the same as Stream Pro.* At equal price the comparison stops being about money and becomes about what you get, which is our best ground.
- No inbound document workflow, no problem-oriented longitudinal chart, no care-gap surfaces.

**The question:** *"If the free one does what you need, what's still taking up your evenings?"* — it's usually not the note. It's the paperwork around it.

**On price, say this:** *"If all you want is a note, take the free one — genuinely. We're not a cheaper scribe, we're a different product. The question is whether the chart is worth solving too."* Conceding the scribe comparison openly is what makes the rest of the conversation credible.

**Watch:** their Feb 2026 price rise from ~$99 to $150 annoyed a lot of small practices. Anyone who budgeted at $99 is now a switcher, and at $150 we're at parity.

## Twofold

**Price:** $49/user/mo billed annually, $69 month-to-month. Unlimited notes — no per-note, per-minute, or per-action metering.
**Wins with:** independent practices and outpatient clinics — **explicitly our ICP, at a third of our price.** Also sells into therapy.
**Their pitch:** unlimited everything, transparent pricing, CPT/ICD-10 coding, BAA, style learning, EHR integration.

**Where they genuinely beat us**
- **Price, badly.** $49 against $149. They also bundle coding, which we charge Pro pricing for.
- No audio stored — a clean privacy answer that plays well with cautious buyers.
- They claim EHR integration; we don't have any.

**Where we win**
- Same category argument as everywhere else: it is a very good, very cheap note generator. No problem-oriented chart, no inbound document pipeline, no care-gap or HCC surfaces, no patient chart to speak of.
- At $49 they are competing on price in a market where price is collapsing. That's a race we shouldn't enter and they can't exit.

**The question:** *"At $49 unlimited, what happens to the notes after they're written?"*

**Handle the price gap head-on.** Don't defend $149 as a scribe price — it isn't one, and the prospect can do arithmetic. *"If you want the cheapest good scribe, it's probably them. We're priced like a chart because that's what we are."*

**Watch this one for a second reason.** Twofold runs a large SEO content operation — most of the "best AI scribe 2026" and competitor-pricing comparison pages that rank for our category are theirs, including pages targeting competitors by name. They are out-publishing the market on exactly the buying-intent keywords we care about. That's a marketing problem more than a sales one, but reps should know that a prospect who "did some research" has probably read Twofold's framing of the landscape.

## Doximity Scribe

**Price:** **Free** to verified US clinicians.
**Wins with:** essentially every US physician, because they already have a Doximity account.
**Their pitch:** free, trusted brand, already in your pocket.

**Where they genuinely beat us**
- Free, and zero acquisition friction — no procurement, no trial, no new vendor.
- Brand trust. Doximity is not a startup to them.

**Where we win**
- No EHR integration either (they copy-paste, same as us — so that objection is neutral here, not a loss).
- English only.
- It is a note generator and nothing more: no chart, no patients, no documents, no tasks, no coding.
- The recording is discarded after the summary — nothing accumulates, ever.

**The question:** *"When you use Doximity Scribe, where does the note go?"*

**Don't fight the price.** You cannot beat free with a discount. The play is category: free solves the note; nothing solves the chart. If the prospect only wants a note, Doximity is a fine answer and we should let them have it — they're not our buyer today, and a graceful exit gets you referred later.

## Abridge & Microsoft/Nuance DAX Copilot

*(One card — different companies, same sales situation.)*

**Price:** Abridge is enterprise-only, no self-serve, no public pricing; third-party estimates run ~$200–$1,200+/provider/mo. DAX Copilot is sold through health systems and Epic.
**Wins with:** health systems and large multi-specialty groups.
**Their pitch:** deepest EHR integration, KLAS scores, enterprise credibility.

**Where they genuinely beat us**
- Real, deep, bidirectional EHR integration — especially DAX with Epic. This is not close.
- Enterprise credibility, security attestations, procurement-ready.
- Very large deployments and published outcomes.

**Where we win — but read the situation first**
These usually come up as *"my friend at the hospital uses Abridge"*, not as an actual competing quote. An independent 4-clinician practice generally cannot buy either one: no self-serve signup, no published price, and an implementation designed for a health system.

**The question:** *"Did they quote you, or is that what the hospital across town uses?"* It's almost always the second, and the conversation ends there.

**If they genuinely have a quote:** they're bigger than our ICP, they likely have an IT department, and per the field sheets they're probably a disqualify. Say so honestly and don't burn the hour.

## Suki

**Price:** roughly $299–$399+/provider/mo depending on plan and commitment.
**Wins with:** small and mid practices who want a scribe with real EHR connectivity and will pay for it.
**Their pitch:** ambient scribe plus voice commands, with EHR integrations.

**Where they genuinely beat us**
- EHR integrations we don't have.
- Voice command surface.

**Where we win**
- **Price.** This is the one card where we're the cheaper option — roughly half. Lead with it.
- Still a scribe. Same category argument, and at 2–3× our price.

**The question:** *"At $300 a month, what are you getting beyond the note?"*

## Tali AI (Canada)

**Price:** free tier; Pro at CAD $100/user/mo annual, CAD $135 monthly.
**Wins with:** Canadian primary care. Effectively the default there.
**Their pitch:** Canadian-built, integrated directly into the EMRs Canadian clinics actually run.

**Where they genuinely beat us**
- **Direct EMR integration with the Canadian stack** — Oscar Pro, Accuro, PS Suite, Med Access, CHR, Healthquest, Profile. Not "in development." Live.
- A free tier on top of that.
- **Canada Health Infoway's AI Scribe Program is funding up to 10,000 one-year licences for eligible primary care clinicians.** In much of Canada a competing scribe is not merely cheaper than us — it is free, integrated, and government-funded.
- Broader adjacent surface: task management, fillable forms, clinical search, call-a-patient.

**Where we win**
- The same category argument holds — it's a scribe plus utilities, not a longitudinal problem-oriented chart with document ingestion.
- Our HCC and risk-adjustment surfaces are US-specific and don't translate, so the win has to be made purely on chart structure and inbound documents.

**The honest read: Canada is not a market we should be spending outbound effort on right now.** Free + natively integrated + publicly funded is three structural disadvantages at once, and we have none of the three. If a Canadian prospect comes to us inbound, sell the chart and the fax pile. Don't prospect into it.

## The EHR's own scribe (Elation Note Assist, eCW Sunoh, Athena ambient)

**This is the most underrated threat on this page and the one most likely to actually kill a deal.**

**Price:** bundled or near-free as an add-on to software they already pay for.
**Their pitch:** it's already in your EHR, there's nothing to buy, nothing to log into.

**Where they genuinely beat us**
- Zero extra login, zero extra vendor, zero extra invoice.
- The note lands in the chart natively. No paste. This is the double-documentation objection, and against this competitor we lose it outright.
- Procurement is a checkbox.

**Where we win**
- Note quality is usually noticeably worse — these are bolt-ons, not the company's core product.
- The chart it writes into is still the EHR's flat, document-shaped chart. Nothing is organized by problem, nothing carries forward, faxes are still a pile.
- No care-gap surfaces that a clinician actually sees at the visit — EHR recall reports are retrospective and land in a report nobody opens.

**The question:** *"Pull up your EHR's recall report while we're on the call. Two things — does it know the difference between a visit that's clinically due and one your payer will actually cover yet, and does it reach the clinician at the visit or land in a report nobody opens?"*

**Be honest about the paste.** Give it two sentences — one click, formats into any field, section by section — and move on. Arguing a fair objection makes it bigger.

## Status quo (no scribe at all)

Still the most common competitor, and the easiest room. There's no feature comparison, only inertia and AI skepticism.

- Skeptic → objection-map category 5. Lead with provenance: every claim from an outside document links to the exact sentence it came from, and anything unverifiable is suppressed. Show it; don't describe it.
- Burned-out → category 2.
- "Too busy to switch" → the 30-day trial, no card, nothing to install.

---

## When to walk away

Reps who disqualify quickly close more. Hand it back if:

- **They require EHR write-back as a condition.** We don't have it and won't this year. Say so plainly.
- **They're health-system owned or have an IT department.** Not our product.
- **They need SOC 2 to sign.** We don't have it. Don't promise a date.
- **Under 3 clinicians.** Route to the self-serve trial per the booking floor — it's not a loss, it's a different door.
- **They only want a note and are happy paying $0–79.** Heidi Free, Doximity, or Freed genuinely serve them better today. A clean "we're not your answer yet" gets you referrals; a forced trial gets you a churn.

## What would make this document real

Everything above is reasoned from our content, public pricing, and market structure. **It is not built from our own loss data, because we don't capture any** — as of 2026-08-19 there are 94 closed-lost deals in HubSpot and `closed_lost_reason` is empty on every one.

Two changes would replace inference with evidence:

1. **Required close-lost reason** on the deal record, with a competitor picklist. Five-minute HubSpot config.
2. **Ask "what are you using today?" on every qualifying call** and log the answer. Within a quarter this document rewrites itself from data, and we'd know which of these seven cards actually matter.

Until then, treat the ordering here as a hypothesis. My guess is that Freed, Heidi, and the EHR-native scribes are 80% of what reps actually hit, and that Abridge and DAX are name-drops rather than real competition — but that is a guess, and it's a cheap one to replace.
